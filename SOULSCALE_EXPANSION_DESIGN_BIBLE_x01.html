Soulscale: Purgatory — Expansion Design Bible

Expansion rules x0.1 · designed against the supplied v1.0.1 rulebook · 11 November 2026

Deliverable: a complete first playable expansion specification, including 96 new card definitions, six supplied Mythic concepts plus two Human Mythics, Civil War, Human Rebellion, a campaign and invention framework, sample decks, and adjudication examples.

Status: proposed game design, not a shipped game update. None of these cards has been added to the Python application. No implementation source was supplied. Numerical balance remains a playtest hypothesis; the existing rulebook itself describes its pool as a first playtest draft. This document does not claim tournament validation.

Read this first

Soulscale works best when a joke creates a tactical decision. A stuck progress bar should delay something identifiable. A pointless login loop should tempt somebody to try again. A tiny mercy should give a player room to act. The system below makes those ideas behave differently rather than giving every joke another +1.

Start with the two all-new teaching decks in section 11, Standard ±18, and no Mythics. Introduce the themed sets, then Borrowed Grace, then Mythics. Civil War and Human Rebellion are alternate match formats. The campaign surrounds matches and never silently changes their rules.

The purgatory discussions, recursive “proofs,” and speculative QR explanations in the source files are used as fiction and comic imagery. They are not adopted as factual evidence about reality or as verified technical findings. Real substances supply some working Mythic names; the mechanics describe fictional tempo, not pharmacology. Both original and alternative display titles are provided.

2. Expansion bridge: exact rules and timings

These rulings apply to the Expansion format only. Preserve an unmodified v1.0.1 mode. Several source rules are summaries rather than executable definitions; these decisions close those gaps instead of pretending they were already present in the prototype.

2.1 Identity and card records

Every player has a permanent controller ID. Every card has a unique card-definition ID and each deck slot has a distinct physical-copy ID. Every token, trap, condition, scheduled packet, and debt records its controller, source card, source Chamber, creation round, and expiry. Faction is Heaven, Hell, or Human; controller is the person who owns that object. An opponent is always another controller, even when factions match.

New cards have explicit base Power P, base movement M, stack S, aspect, legal Chambers, movement type, keywords, costs, and effect text. M is an unsigned magnitude. Neutral M=0 does not become scoring movement from intensity, Press, Release, or buffs. For these new cards, P starts at 1 except Mythics. All special numerical bonuses are printed; descriptive type names confer no hidden powers. No new card has the disruptive flag; their printed Sever bonuses are exhaustive.

Legacy cards keep their real coded stats and aspects if those are available. The supplied rulebook does not print complete base Power/aspect data or every special magnitude for all 40 old cards; do not reconstruct them by guesswork. The all-new sample decks avoid that dependency. In an eventual implementation, import the canonical old definitions, then apply only the bridge’s documented format rules.

Deckbuilding: exactly 10 slots, cost Common=1, Uncommon=2, Rare=3, Mythic=4; total ≤24; Rare plus Mythic slots ≤4; Mythic slots ≤1. Non-Mythic duplicates are legal. A Mythic has no duplicates. Civil and Human legality is checked before cost. Card IDs, not emoji, identify new cards: emoji already collide in the base pool.

At each reveal, uniformly sample four distinct physical slots from the ten, without replacement within that reveal. Return every slot to the pool after the round: there is no draw/discard cycle. A card may recur next round. This is an explicit Expansion-format reveal rule, because the written v1.0.1 document does not specify its reshuffle policy. Set all randomness from a logged seed in digital play; tabletop shuffle the ten, show four, then reunite the ten before the next draw.

2.3 Commitments and costs

Both players commit their ordinary card, Chamber, Intent, Release spend, any optional payments, and all targets before either rolls. Existing public board state can be inspected. Only committed IDs can be targeted. An effect cannot select a new target after seeing the opposing commitment unless its text explicitly says so. All cards here use commitment targets. A player may voluntarily Pass.

Will is 3 plus due bonuses minus due penalties, clamped 0–6. Unused Will expires. Pay in this order: Edict cost; commitment Tax/Fine Print payments; Heavy; optional intensity reroll; reserved project payments after resolution. You must reserve project Will in advance, so it cannot be used for a reroll. Heavy always takes 1 if available; otherwise -1 force. A Tax is optional: pay at commitment or lose 1 force per unpaid Tax, capped at 2 unpaid-Tax force loss. Fine Print is also pay 1 or lose 1 force, independently. You can commit a card with no Will; apply unpaid penalties rather than deadlocking the match.

Reserve a Release spend of 0 to all available friendly stack in the chosen Chamber. The amount is fixed before rolls; reserved stack is unavailable for Hungry or any other same-round spending/transfer. Remove reserved Release stack even if the play fizzles. This is an intentional commitment risk. Enemy Sever cannot reduce the already computed Release bonus. Non-Release card costs and opponentward costs are paid even when a play fizzles. Other benefits require surviving with Power ≥1. Printed pre-clash weakening is the exception: generate it once from the committed glyph, even if that glyph later fizzles. Do not recalculate such weakening recursively.

If a lock forbids a Chamber, you cannot commit an ordinary or Bonus play there. Several locks on one Chamber still last only their stated rounds. If all reveals are illegal, Pass; never choose an illegal Chamber just to keep the game running.

2.4 Roll, force, and Intents

Roll one d6 per active play: 1–2→I=1, 3–4→I=2, 5–6→I=3. One optional reroll per play costs 1 Will. Every raw intensity 6, including a discarded first roll, adds 1 shared Strain. Wild or event rolls are not intensity rolls. All dice for a window are recorded before resolving that window.

Force +n means add n to both Power and a scoring card’s active Amount. Force -n reduces both, each floored at 0. For M=0 cards, force changes Power only; it cannot invent movement. Power-only effects change Power only. No effect changes I after the roll.

Start with Power=P+I and, if M>0, Amount=M+I. For Press, add the base formula’s bonus to both Power and scoring Amount, now capped at 3. Harmony H is the number of friendly logical stack tokens of the matching aspect at commitment, not their total value. Formula: max(0,min(3,floor((F+H-floor(E/2))/3))). New source token batches count as one token each; partial removal keeps that token until value reaches 0. Base card aspects require canonical data.

Anchor: Power -1, active output 0, and stack addition S+I+(1 if H>0 else 0). Release: bonus B=min(5,ceil(spend×I/4)); add B to Power and scoring Amount; add no stack. Sever: Power -1, scoring Amount -1; gain +1 Power if E>0; cut S+I enemy stack (plus a printed bonus), and add max(1,S-1) friendly stack. Press adds S friendly stack. All stacking/cutting is contingent on the play not fizzling; reserved Release spending is not.

Use the base Heavy, Fragile, Volatile, Binding, Hungry, Delayed, Echo, and Desperate rules. Hungry consumes 1 unreserved friendly stack and adds +1 force, or loses 1 force if none; it does not trigger with Release. Volatile uses the kept roll; a kept 1–2 adds 1 Strain and -2 force, a kept 5–6 adds +1 force. Fragile means -1 force if an enemy active play targets the Chamber, even if that play later fizzles. “Ahead” is progress toward your assigned goal; for celestial Human-Rebellion opponents use their own signed track goal. Human glyphs in this catalogue do not use Desperate.

Apply Intent and all force/Power adjustments from the same public commitment snapshot. Evaluate conditional text when it says to; otherwise at commitment. Sum all numeric Power deltas, sum all Amount deltas, floor each at 0 once. Fizzle if final Power=0. A surviving play may have Amount=0; it can still add stack or cleanse. Existing guards/traps apply before clash. Newly installed objects cannot retroactively weaken the current active play.

Same-Chamber opposing plays clash: lower-Power Amount halves; a tie halves both; floor each. A clash does not halve stack addition, Sever cuts, cleanses, fixed costs, or ongoing movement. It does not cancel a special merely because its controller lost. M=0 still contests with Power. Different Chambers do not clash.

The dawn and dusk are distinct movement windows. A round cap applies independently to the net in each window; a next-round cap persists for both windows of that round, then expires. Thus a Stasis round allows at most 2 in either direction at dawn and at most 2 at dusk. This is a new explicit cap ruling. Multiple caps combine using the narrowest allowed interval. A directional cap limits only that direction of net movement, not every source packet. Local gates and firewalls instead limit a named packet before netting.

All output in one movement window is simultaneous: a +3 and -3 at Soul +17 net to 0, with no transient Heaven win. Fixed cost movement also joins its window’s net. Clip the final Soul to the selected edges. The normal verdict follows events, matching the base sequence; no new event here moves the Soul directly.

Batch order for board mutations: (1) remove reserved spends and Hungry costs; (2) remove committed existing objects and apply enemy stack cuts to the remaining pre-existing stack; (3) apply voluntary self-cuts/transfers, limited to what remains; (4) add new stack; (5) install/refresh objects and personal permissions. Cuts never eat stack created later in the same batch. A target removed by another effect is simply absent; no retargeting or refund. Simultaneous opposite cleanses can both work. A surviving glyph can install its new object even if a cleanse removed its old same-ID object. New objects cannot be targeted by commitments in this round.

For multiple cuts, shields absorb the first eligible cut; order equal-window cuts by source card ID and physical-copy ID, never by player seat. Default stack removal is oldest token first, then source ID; Release may instead reserve specific tokens at commitment. Transfer moves surviving pre-existing token value only, and cannot duplicate it. Opposite movement always nets; stack never changes controller except where a card explicitly transfers ownership (none here).

2.6 Strain and original event compatibility

The six original events remain. Collapse/Reprieve: the next eligible ordinary ownerward movement packet of the named faction gains 2; one-use, no expiry. Intensification: both ordinary plays in the next commitment batch gain +1 force, then consume the event; this symmetrical bridge replaces ambiguous “next active effect” seat ordering. Multiple copies add. Flashback: remove Memory conditions and cut 2 pre-existing Memory stack per controller. Stasis: next round net cap [-2,+2] at each movement window. Judgement: the controller farther from its win goal gains +2 Will next round; a tie gives each +1.

In Civil War, map Collapse to the negative-goal controller and Reprieve to the positive-goal controller. In Rebellion, a faction-specific boost with no controller of that faction becomes +1 next-round Will for the Human instead; a boost for the actual celestial faction remains +2 to its next eligible ordinary ownerward packet. Judgement gives the Human +2 Will if Agency<3, otherwise the celestial +2; this is a bespoke scenario rule. Flashback also cuts Human Memory stack. All Strain events are optional only as an entire pre-agreed no-events teaching variant; never remove an inconvenient individual event after rolling it.

3. New effects, movement packets, and durations

Movement component rule. A card’s default movement type applies to its scoring output. A printed fixed cost or scheduled trap output is a separate component with its own movement type. The engine must not coerce every component to the top-level type. Example: False Mercy has neutral primary output, a fixed 2 opponentward cost, and a later fixed 3 ownerward bait packet. This extends the v1.0.1 concept; its current single-field resolver alone cannot express that whole card safely.

Output for a Mythic multiplier means the ordinary glyph’s one direct movement Amount after clash, before direction. If Delayed, multiply when scheduled, store the final amount, and never multiply again at dawn. Fixed costs, traps, Echo, Wild extra packets, ongoing movement, and event-only movement are not multiplied. Bonuses that already contributed to the ordinary Amount are included once. Stack, cuts, Power, Will, conditions, project progress, and cleanse counts are never multiplied. This bounded interpretation replaces the unsafe proposal to multiply “everything.”

Echo schedules 1 ownerward at next dawn if a surviving non-Delayed ordinary glyph produced nonzero direct movement after local suppression, even if an enemy packet later cancelled it in the net. Echo does not Echo itself. In this catalogue no glyph combines Echo and Delayed. A base glyph that does so schedules its primary delayed packet and one Echo for the same next dawn; both use their recorded directions and neither recurses.

Object slots and refresh: an ongoing, guard, trap, or Chamber condition is one object. An object may have several printed functions but occupies one slot. A new same-card, same-controller object here refreshes that object and its expiry; it never stacks its numeric effect. Installing a third object requires committing which existing friendly object will be replaced. If no replacement is committed, the new object is not installed; other parts of the glyph still work. A removed replacement target leaves space normally. Personal next-play permissions, locks, Mythic states, and due packets are not objects and do not occupy slots. Repeated same-source permissions refresh, never stack; distinct permissions add subject to the stated ceilings.

Default duration: traps and new persistent conditions last through the end of r+2 unless their text names a shorter interval. One-round Taxes, gates, and next-play debuffs target r+1 only. New ongoing/guard objects say “this and next round,” so they trigger/protect through r+1, then expire at next dawn. A condition expressly for “this and next” follows that duration. A permissions deadline such as “through r+2” is inclusive. Durations count calendar rounds, including mandatory Pass rounds. Legacy ongoing objects retain their legacy indefinite duration, within the new object-slot ceiling.

Tax: a controller-marked condition in one Chamber. At commitment next round, the affected controller may pay 1 Will for a play there; otherwise that play loses 1 force. It expires after that round. It never taxes an Edict or a project. Refreshing does not accumulate the same-source Tax. Fine Print is a separately named Tax-like condition.

Hold: a visible condition in one Chamber, valid at the next dawn only. Choose at installation the enemy due packet ID if one is already public, or commit the selection rule “largest eligible packet, ties oldest then source ID.” An eligible packet is delayed, belongs to the enemy, originates here, is due at that dawn, and has never been Held. Up to 2 points are removed from its amount and become a new packet due one dawn later; the rest resolves now. Mark both parts Held. If multiple Holds contend, the first by creation round then source ID acts; others find it marked and do nothing. Consume Hold at that dawn even if nothing qualified. Echo, cost, project, trap, and Mythic debts cannot be Held. A 5-point packet becomes 3 now and 2 next dawn, not 5 postponed forever.

Trap: an installed trap watches from the next defence/clash window onward. The first eligible enemy active play here triggers it, even if that play then fizzles. A post-play loop/bait trap marks its trigger during defence and registers its delayed packet after that play; cleansing during the active batch cannot undo the registered trigger. Bonus plays can trigger traps. Where a trap specifically says “ordinary,” Bonus plays do not trigger it. Multiple traps trigger in age/source-ID order, within the combined force-loss cap; all triggered ordinary force traps are consumed even if the cap absorbs their extra penalty. A trap packet is fixed, unscaled, non-Echo, and can resolve after the source trap is gone.

Guard: use the largest enemy guard strength in that Chamber, never their sum. It reduces force of each enemy active play before clash. A newly installed guard starts protecting next round; installing one never cancels a current attack. Cleansing a guard during active resolution affects future defence only.

Ongoing: new ongoing effects produce their printed 1 movement at dusk of installation and the next round. Friendly stack 6+ adds 1, as in base, checked after all stack mutations. A Human ward removes 1 after this bonus. Multiple enemy wards with the same function use the largest reduction, not their sum. General Strike then suppresses up to 4 remaining total ongoing points according to its committed allocation; excess allocation is lost. A removed ongoing has no dusk packet; removing it cannot erase an earlier dawn packet.

Cost movement: fixed opponentward prices cannot be multiplied, halved by clash, erased by Anchor, reduced by guards/firewalls, or cleansed. They still join the final movement net and obey global movement caps. They can lose you the game. If a player voluntarily commits a cost, pay it even if the glyph fizzles. The four sacrifice/bait glyphs have no primary scoring output, so their Intents change Power and Resonance only.

4. Mythics: borrowing tomorrow

4.1 Access and the Edict window

A Mythic occupies one of the ten deck slots and costs 4 rarity points; at most one. To activate it, it must be in this round’s four-card reveal, be unspent, and have an affordable printed cost. Reveal it in the Edict window before ordinary commitments and rolls. It does not use a Chamber or Intent and is not an active play. The other revealed slots remain available for your ordinary play. Its effect is public before either player commits that play. Both players’ Edicts reveal together; there is no counter-response chain.

An unused Mythic can instead be played as Ordinary Inspiration: P1/M1/S1, aspect Clarity, any Chamber, ownerward; for a Human it is P1/M0/S2, neutral. This fallback has Common-strength stats but the slot still costs 4 in deckbuilding. Using fallback does not spend the Edict. After activation, flip the slot to Spent Inspiration with the identical fallback stats for all later reveals. This keeps ten usable slots without endlessly recurring a once-per-match effect. Do not separately add fallback cards to a deck.

Activating requires no Chamber, so Binding cannot stop it. You may activate during a mandatory Pass round, but Pass still forbids your ordinary and Bonus plays. It is not stoppable by an ordinary cleanse, Sever, guard, or trap. The defence is to exploit its announced window, evade its affected output, grow infrastructure, or push before repayment. Debt is a player-level obligation, not a board object. Only match end clears unpaid debt.

4.2 Multiplier composition and skipped rounds

Compose applicable multipliers as one rational number, clamp the product to [1/2,2], then floor each ordinary output packet once. Thus ×3/2 combined with ×1/2 yields ×3/4; Amount 5 becomes floor(15/4)=3. Two ×1/2 effects clamp to ×1/2 rather than ×1/4. No intermediate rounding. No Mythic changes clash Power.

Mandatory Pass means no ordinary or Bonus glyph, no intensity roll, no Intent, no new stacks from an active glyph. Will refresh, reveals, Edicts, scheduled packets, locks, duration clocks, ongoing effects, Strain events, and cost debts all continue. A Human may not complete projects in a mandatory Pass round. Multiple Pass obligations due the same round merge into one Pass; they do not move to a later round. A Bonus opportunity on a mandatory Pass round is lost and its later debt remains. A voluntary Pass never pre-pays future debt.

4.3 Overtime is a Bonus play, not a new round

Overtime Requisition preserves the idea of two actions next time without rewinding the whole turn engine. At r+1 commit two distinct physical non-Mythic revealed slots in different legal Chambers. Both use Press. Label one ordinary and one Bonus before seeing the enemy play. Each rolls its own intensity and may reroll using remaining Will. A duplicate definition is allowed only if two physical copies were actually revealed. You may use only the ordinary play if no legal Bonus exists; the future Pass is still owed.

The enemy play clashes with whichever of the two is in its Chamber; the other does not clash. Both have normal costs, direct output, printed specials, and Press stack. The Bonus cannot activate a Mythic, grant another Bonus, inherit any Mythic multiplier, or cause another round/reveal/Will refresh. Direct output from the Bonus is capped at 3 after clash. Its Delayed primary packet is also capped at 3 when scheduled. Echo and other printed fixed specials work once normally. Resolve mutations through the same active batch. An enemy force trap can trigger on the Bonus; an ordinary-only trap cannot.

4.5 Human Mythics

General Strike of One Soul: suppress up to 4 points from committed existing enemy ongoing objects at this dusk. Pay 2 Will and add 1 Strain; no projects in r+1 or r+2. This is an emergency brake, not permanent immunity. New ongoing objects the opponent installs this round cannot be selected retroactively.

We Built Our Own Exit: at this verdict you may complete two projects in different Chambers, each at its full ordinary cost. Pay 2 Will for the Edict plus reserve 2 Will per intended project; completing both therefore requires 6 Will. No projects in r+1 or r+2. The Edict itself generates no Agency and bypasses no central-band test, stack requirement, edge verdict, per-Chamber limit, or total Agency threshold. It can finish an established position; it cannot create a free victory from empty Chambers.

Both Human Mythics require prior agreement because the opposing celestial has no legal shared Mythic in this initial Rebellion package. First test Human Rebellion without either, then measure the optional package separately.

5. Civil War: angels against angels, demons against demons

Choose Heaven–Heaven or Hell–Hell. Randomly assign one controller goal +G and the other -G, independently of faction. G is the usual 12/18/24 setting. All friendly stack, harmony, guards, traps, cleansing, and buffs belong to controllers. The endpoint is a claim to jurisdiction over the Soul, not a claim that an angel became a demon. Positive means the positive-goal player’s claim; negative the other’s.

ownerward follows the controller’s assigned endpoint; opponentward follows the opponent’s endpoint. For legacy absolute heavenward/hellward effects, positive/negative remains literal. Card text that says “a Devil effect” or “an Angel play” in the original pool becomes “an enemy effect/play” in Civil War. Own-faction buffs apply only to the controlling player, never both same-faction players. Ahead/behind compares Soul to 0 in the controller’s own goal direction. Body stays Body; no Chamber is renamed.

Use faction-matching base/themed cards, the six matching Civil War cards, and optionally one shared Mythic. You cannot import the other faction’s cards merely because controller identity now differs. The twelve Civil cards are not legal in ordinary Heaven–Hell. All ordinary ceilings, events with the mapping above, caps, and timings apply.

Targeting examples: a negative-goal angel playing Candle contributes negative ownerward movement; its Guardian weakens the other angel’s play. A positive-goal demon’s ownerward packet is positive. False Mercy pays toward its rival’s endpoint. There are no shared friendly Resonance pools and no faction-wide immunity to friendly-looking cleanses.

Balance practice: play two games, swapping assigned endpoints and first-seat UI. Random seed may be paired for tests, but do not publish the second game’s hidden future reveals to a participant. Theoretically symmetric movement is not proof that every implementation order is symmetric.

6. Human Rebellion: the Soul gets a vote

6.1 Format and victory

Two players: Human versus one celestial controller, either Heaven or Hell. Use Standard ±18 for the default Rebellion format; Quick/Deep are optional variants that need separate balance results. Soul starts at 0. The celestial retains its ordinary signed victory goal: Heaven +18 or Hell -18. The unused edge is not a win condition for anybody and remains a physical limit. A celestial cannot win by accidentally reaching the opposite edge. The Human has no track endpoint and never generates ownerward/opponentward movement; its catalogue is neutral.

The Human wins with 6 Agency, earned through projects in at least three Chambers. Each Chamber can hold at most two completed projects. Project markers are permanent achievements; they are not stack or board objects and cannot be Severed, cleansed, stolen, or tax-targeted. They do not generate movement. This supplies a finite victory objective independent of how much discomfort an adversary created.

At the verdict, celestial edge victory takes precedence. If the celestial has not won, a Human may complete a precommitted project if all of these are true:

  1. Soul is within [-6,+6] after dusk and events.
  2. The project Chamber contains at least 5 Human stack after the active batch.
  3. Spend exactly 3 of that Human stack, oldest first unless tokens were reserved at commitment.
  4. Spend the 2 Will reserved for that project at commitment.
  5. The Human is not in a mandatory Pass or project-lock round.
  6. The Chamber has fewer than two completed projects.

Award 1 Agency and place a permanent project marker in that Chamber. Normal limit: one completed project each round. Only the Human Exit Mythic permits two, in different Chambers. If a precommitted project is no longer legal, do not spend its stack or reserved Will; the opportunity is lost and unused Will expires. Only project Will is reserved in advance. Project stack is checked and spent at verdict from whatever remains, including stack added this round; there is no reservation of not-yet-existing stack. Existing Release reservations and earlier Hungry/transfer spending take precedence, so double spending is impossible. Edict Will is paid first. No project can be declared after seeing the final Soul position.

On earning Agency 6, Human wins immediately after valid projects. If neither wins by the end of round 30, the celestial wins by containment. Rebellion therefore cannot be won by indefinite turtling. Agency alone is insufficient if the central-band and construction requirements cannot be met.

6.2 How neutral Human play interacts

Human Press builds stack and contests clashes; its Press bonus changes Power, not Soul movement. Human Anchor gives S+I+(harmony?1:0) stack and -1 Power. Human Release spends stack for clash Power only, adds no new stack, and produces no movement: use it to blunt an approaching burst at a real construction cost. Human Sever cuts enemy infrastructure using the normal formula and adds its reduced stack. Human guards can halve or fizzle a hostile direct play through Power/clash; they do not automatically erase all movement outside their Chamber.

The celestial can target Human stack with Sever and can target Human guards/conditions with any legally applicable cleanse. In Rebellion, legacy text naming the opposing faction means enemy controller, including Human; otherwise a whole class of base answers would fail merely because the Human lacks a halo. Human glyphs never change faction or become claimants to a heavenly/hellish edge.

The Human’s stack is friendly only to itself. Friendly 6-stack ongoing bonuses never benefit the celestial from Human infrastructure. Project-spent stack is gone and can lower future defence. A completed project does not raise a Chamber’s stack cap. Three developed Chambers provide the natural minimum for six Agency; two overprotected Chambers cannot solve the game alone.

6.3 Celestial incentives and first playtest

The celestial must decide between pushing toward its edge and visiting Human construction Chambers to contest Anchors or Sever stack. The Human must choose between stacking, clearing passive engines, and reserving 2 Will to build. Guard at the wrong Chamber does little. This creates a distinct game using the existing board rather than a third tug-of-war claimant with no mathematical endpoint.

For first tests, use the Human starter and a teaching celestial deck below, no Mythics, no career boons, Standard ±18, ordinary Strain events. Log project attempts as well as successes. The main uncertainty is the race between six projects and celestial pressure; tune Agency thresholds or central-band width only after observing the race, not by letting an AI improvise a ruling during play.

9.2 Optional literary review

An AI or human reviewer may give 0–2 in each category: clarity, originality relative to this session’s submitted ideas, thematic fit, and comic/emotional payoff. Print the four scores and a one-sentence reason each. These scores are commentary, not damage or ranked XP. All compliant submissions earn the same optional campaign participation Glory. This avoids making model verbosity, faction preference, or prompt tricks a competitive stat.

For a judged writing event separate from a card match: show anonymised entries in shuffled order, use a fixed published rubric, perform a second review in reversed order, and send disagreements of >1 point in any category to a named human judge agreed before the event. If no human judge exists, show both reviews and declare a tie rather than fabricating objective certainty. Never let review text execute instructions, retrieve private match hands, or modify authoritative game state.

Offline alternative: the same template choice and human-written narration; no network or model is required. If generation fails, show the exact mechanical log and the player's own flavour text. A failed narrator must never hold up the turn clock or change a result.

9.3 Minimal AI content envelope

Store only match-public event summaries for narration. The narrator receives card IDs, resolved public actions, permitted NPC flavour, and an explicit statement that player writing is quoted data. It returns a short narration and optional rubric record. Validate field types and length; ignore unsolicited mechanics. Hidden reveals, commitments, seeds, account data, and secret opponent state stay out of narration prompts. An opponent AI choosing a move receives its own reveal and public board only; it must commit before the human choice is disclosed. Do not train a changing scoring policy during competitive matches.

This preserves the useful ideas in game_research.txt—hybrid deterministic rules, traceability, bias checks, and offline fallbacks—without relying on the file’s unverified market statistics or claiming LLM judging is intrinsically fair.

10. Multiplayer without an undefined new combat engine

10.1 The Council: complete 2–6 player session format

The current one-Soul, two-endpoint combat system naturally supports two claimants. For the first multiplayer release, use a Council league of ordinary duels, not six people all ambiguously owning the same positive score. Register 2–6 players, fixed ten-card decks for the session, and one agreed format: Heaven–Hell pairs, Heaven Civil War, Hell Civil War, or paired Human-Rebellion exhibition matches. Rebellion results must not share a competitive standings table with ordinary duels; its asymmetric roles need their own table.

For a 2–6 player Civil War league, every unordered pair plays twice, swapping assigned endpoints. Score each game win 3, draw 1, loss 0. Standings: total league points, then head-to-head points among tied players, then number of game wins, then shared placing. There is no random tie-break damage rule. Each player has 2(n−1) games. Pairings can run in parallel if enough tables exist; otherwise play lexicographic player-ID pair order, first game then endpoint-swapped second game. Deck lock persists through both legs. Mythics reset each game, never between rounds.

For a Heaven–Hell session, divide into fixed faction teams before decks lock, with at least one player on each side. Every cross-faction pair plays twice, one game hosted from each player’s UI seat. There are no same-faction games in that schedule. Compare average league points per game for individual standings if team sizes differ; team result is the sum of all points earned by its players divided by its games played. Use exact rational comparison and shared placing on a tie. This lets 3–6 players participate without falsely calling it a symmetric all-play-all duel league.

For a Human-Rebellion exhibition league, each pair plays two games, swapping Human/celestial roles; agree one celestial faction for the session. Score 3/1/0, although Rebellion timeouts are celestial wins rather than draws. Everyone must register a Human deck and a matching celestial deck. Keep separate statistics for Human and celestial wins. Default no Mythics. This is a role-swapped exhibition, not evidence of equal faction strength.

The league provides rivalries, rematches, promotion trials, and social competition now. A simultaneous six-player shared-Soul mode would require a new attribution and action-economy design; it is deliberately not claimed as part of this first playable system.

10.2 Pacts, bounties, audits, and spectators

These older brainstorming ideas have explicit restricted forms here:

  • Open pact: in a casual Council session, two players may publicly promise a match plan for narrative purposes. It has no rules discount, hidden-hand access, shared Will, or binding in-game action. Broken promises earn a recorded comic title; no automatic account or Glory fine. Ranked collusion is not a legal mechanic.
  • Bounty: publish one optional session challenge: “complete a Memory Release,” “win a clash while behind,” or “remove two objects with one glyph.” Each completed challenge grants one cosmetic badge once per player/session, no match movement or league points. Human mode substitutes “complete one project in Memory” for Release. Record public log evidence.
  • Audit: between campaign matches only, a player may opt for an Audit challenge match using case file 6. Winning pays +1 additional Favor; losing pays ordinary rewards. It never reverses a settled Soul result or confiscates earned progress through an AI opinion.
  • Spectators: may choose the next case-file flavour or vote for their favourite invention in an unranked writing event. They cannot grant movement, expose hands, alter costs, or spend another player’s Will.
  • Ghosts of earlier runs: attach a previous public match quip and one canonical invention reskin as opponent flavour. Do not copy a real person’s private conversation into an NPC.

This keeps the rivalry and bureaucracy while dropping the original suggestion that arbitrary divine audits invert a board state. A completed tactical success should not be erased by a narrator’s whim.

11.2 Bait can backfire

Round 4, Soul +15, ordinary Heaven–Hell. Hell commits False Mercy in Heart with Anchor and pays its fixed +2 opponentward cost. I=2 gives Power 1+2−1=2, active movement 0, and stack 2+2=4 if no harmony. Heaven commits a scoring +3 in another Chamber. No other output: net +5, Soul clips to +18 and Heaven wins at verdict. Hell’s newly installed trap never gets its next-round opportunity. Anchor did not erase the +2 price.

If instead the round ends safely and Heaven Presses into Heart next round, the bait trap registers a fixed -3 for the following dawn. Heaven can avoid it by choosing another Chamber, use Anchor or Sever here to consume it without the bait packet, or remove it in an earlier round. Cleansing it after triggering the same round is too late. This is a decision, not a guaranteed delayed nuke.

11.3 Multipliers are rounded once

Heaven has ordinary post-clash Amount 5. Borrowed Momentum ×3/2 and an enemy Velvet Siege ×1/2 both apply: product 3/4; floor(5×3/4)=3. Heaven’s Power, stack, cuts, and cleanse count stay unchanged. 11.4 Overtime still permits defence

Hell activates Overtime at r=3. At r=4 it reveals two legal physical glyph slots and commits PETTY-01 in Mind as ordinary and AUTH-01 in Time as Bonus, both Press. Heaven plays in Mind, so it clashes only with PETTY-01. Suppose the ordinary Amount becomes 1 after losing the clash and the Bonus Amount would be 4: the Bonus caps to 3. Total direct Hell contribution is -4 before other packets. Both surviving glyphs may build their printed stack and AUTH-01 may install Tax in Time. At r=5 Hell must Pass; the Tax can still apply and existing ongoing effects still tick.

If a lock prohibits one chosen Chamber, that commitment is illegal before rolls. If another Edict forces Hell to Pass in r=4, both plays are lost; the r=5 repayment is still owed. If two Pass obligations meet in r=5, they merge into one calendar-round Pass.

A Heaven Dawn-like packet has magnitude 5, source Time, due at round 6 dawn. Hell’s valid Hold selects it. Resolve +3 now, schedule +2 at round 7 dawn, and mark both parts Held. A second Hold cannot delay either again. If an opposing -3 is also due at round 6 dawn, the window nets to 0. If Stasis applies, cap the combined net after adding all packets; do not separately cap +3 and -3 before summing.

12.1 What has actually been checked

12.4 A practical balance programme

First run 20 teaching games, ten with each player/faction assignment, to find comprehension and timing failures. Then run at least 100 paired-seed games per symmetric format with competent agents or rotating humans, reporting both win rates and draws. Pair seats, endpoints, and faction assignments where applicable. Analyse Human Rebellion separately with role-swapped participants. An automated policy that cannot value projects is not a fair balance test of Rebellion.

Record: format/version/seed; deck IDs and physical slots; round/window; start/end Soul; reveals; commitments; dice/rerolls; Will reserved/spent; pre/post Power and Amount; each movement component and cap; stack additions/cuts/spends; board objects/expiry; Mythic debts; attempted/completed projects; winner/timeout. Record full hidden-state replays privately for audit and export redacted public logs for players while a match is running.

Provisional tuning alarms, not promises: >60% decisive-game win rate for a side after 100 paired games; >20% ordinary draws; >15% mandatory Pass rounds across all played rounds; >30% of games won before round 4; repeated one-card engines doing >40% of a deck’s total useful output; or Human projects succeeding almost regardless of celestial contest. Report sample size and confidence intervals, not a bare percentage.

Change one axis at a time. If Mythics dominate, first raise Will cost or reduce their active window; do not hide a global enemy buff. If taxes lock out play, reduce unpaid penalties or deployment density. If Human wins too easily, test Agency 7 with a newly specified per-Chamber distribution or narrow the safe band; do not silently break the two-project cap. If Humans lose too easily, first lower project threshold 5→4 while retaining the 3-stack spend and six Agency. If loops stall games, shorten object duration or reduce Hold magnitude; never permit recursive unlimited delays.

The catalogue is a coherent starting point for these tests. Balance is empirical, so a fixed rules specification is the first deliverable, not evidence of a finished competitive metagame.

13.3 Resolved design choices

One Mythic per deck is retained, with rarity cost 4 and a once-per-match flip, rather than a free eleventh card. Pre-resolution power is a separate visible Edict window, rather than secretly multiplying a result after dice. Extra turns become one bounded two-glyph round, avoiding a second Will refresh or recursively ticking engines. Same-faction combat follows controllers, not card art. Rebellion has an independent achievable objective instead of a meaningless third sign on a one-dimensional track. Inventions express player wit inside a visible contract; subjective model judgement cannot rewrite tactics. RPG progress unlocks optional campaign choices and presentation, never ranked stat advantages.

The final unresolved question is measured balance. Everything needed to make a first physical prototype is specified here; everything needed to claim a balanced commercial expansion still requires implementation and playtest evidence.

Design confidence, as judgement rather than measured probability: error-free specification/internal consistency 0.91; suitability to the supplied concept 0.96; effectiveness as a first playable design 0.92; efficiency/readability 0.91; completeness for the defined scope 0.93. These assess the specification only. Empirical balance is unmeasured and deliberately receives no invented confidence score.
